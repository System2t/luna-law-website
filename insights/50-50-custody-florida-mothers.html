50/50 Custody in Florida: What Mothers Need to Know

By Hernan M. Luna, Esq. · October 6, 2026 · 2 min read · Pairs with the reel "This One Is for the Mothers"

The short answer

Florida courts decide time-sharing on the best interests of the child under Florida Statute 61.13, not on which parent asks loudest. Since 2023 the law starts from a rebuttable presumption that equal time-sharing is in the child's best interest, but that presumption can be overcome by evidence, and a 50/50 request is not supposed to be a tool for lowering child support.

She carried the baby for nine months, and the responsibility for years after that. Then, when child support becomes part of the conversation, the other parent shows up asking for 50/50.

It is a pattern we see often in Florida time-sharing cases, and it deserves a straight answer.

Is 50/50 time-sharing automatic in Florida?

Not automatically, and it is not supposed to be used as a way to reduce a support obligation. Florida Statute 61.13 directs courts to decide parenting plans and time-sharing based on the best interests of the child, looked at through a specific set of statutory factors. Since 2023 the statute starts from a rebuttable presumption that equal time-sharing is in a child's best interest, but a presumption is a starting point, not a result: it can be overcome by evidence about the child's actual routine, history, and needs. What the law does not reward is a 50/50 request raised to change the child support math.

This is not an argument against fathers

Good fathers who have been present, involved, and responsible deserve to fight for real time with their children, and we represent them too. The issue is when "50/50" is raised as leverage rather than as a genuine parenting arrangement grounded in the child's actual routine, history, and needs.

Three things that matter most for mothers in a time-sharing dispute

Document the history

Who has handled school pickups, doctor's appointments, daily routines, and day-to-day decisions. Florida courts look at the established pattern of caregiving, and that pattern needs to be shown, not just stated.

Preserve the evidence

Text messages, shared calendars, school and medical records, and anything else that reflects the real day-to-day parenting arrangement should be kept and organized, not reconstructed from memory months later.

Protect your child

Decisions about time-sharing should be driven by what is actually stable and workable for the child involved, not by what either parent wants to prove to the other.

Key takeaways

  • Florida decides time-sharing on the child's best interests under Florida Statute 61.13; a 50/50 request is not a way to lower child support.
  • The 2023 presumption of equal time-sharing is rebuttable: the established pattern of caregiving still has to be shown with evidence.
  • Document the history, preserve texts, calendars, and records, and keep the focus on what is stable for the child.
