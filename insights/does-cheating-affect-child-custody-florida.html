Does Cheating Affect Child Custody in Florida?

By Hernan M. Luna, Esq. · October 11, 2026 · 2 min read · Pairs with the Ask Luna reel "Cheating Doesn't Automatically Cost You Your Children in Florida"

The short answer

No, not by itself. Florida courts decide parenting plans on the best interests of the child, and an affair standing alone is not one of the statutory factors a judge weighs. What can matter is how the children were affected: neglect, instability, exposure to multiple partners, or a disrupted routine.

Having an affair, by itself, does not automatically mean a parent loses time-sharing with their children in Florida. That surprises a lot of people, but it is the law.

Why an affair alone is not a custody factor

Florida family courts are directed to focus on the best interests of the child, not on punishing a spouse for being unfaithful. An affair, standing alone, is not one of the statutory factors a judge weighs when deciding a parenting plan.

The catch: what happened because of the affair

Courts do care about what happened because of the affair. That means questions like these can become relevant to a custody determination:

  • Did the relationship lead to the children being neglected?
  • Were the children repeatedly left with other people while the affair was happening?
  • Were the children exposed to multiple romantic partners or unstable situations?
  • Did the parent's choices start affecting the children's routine, stability, safety, or emotional well-being?

Once the conversation moves from "there was an affair" to "here is how the children were impacted," the calculus changes. At that point, a court is not punishing infidelity. It is looking at parenting judgment and the child's stability, which is exactly what Florida law asks it to do.

What about alimony and property division?

This same distinction tends to come up in alimony and equitable distribution discussions too, where the affair itself matters less than any dissipation of marital assets connected to it.

Focus on the facts that touch your children or your finances

If infidelity is part of what is driving your divorce or custody case, the facts that matter are the ones that touch your children or your finances, not the affair in isolation. Call 1-800-868-5862 or message WhatsApp 786-541-0899 for a confidential consultation.

Key takeaways

  • An affair by itself is not a statutory factor in a Florida parenting plan decision.
  • What can matter is the effect on the children: neglect, instability, exposure to multiple partners, or a disrupted routine.
  • In alimony and property division, the question is dissipation of marital assets, not the affair in isolation.
